There is a pair of white sneakers by the front door. They have been by the front door, in roughly the same position, for long enough that the doormat has a faint grey shadow where the right toe rests between trips. The leather — for they are leather, not canvas, which is the first clue that someone in this household actually thought about what they were buying — has gone the color of old ivory. The laces, which were once a brilliant optic white, have softened to the tone of unbleached linen. The heel of the left one has a small scuff from the night they skated on a wet curb. There is, on the inner side of the right one, a faint blue mark that came, no one quite remembers how, from walking too close to a pair of new jeans.

They are not, by any reasonable definition, a new pair of sneakers. And they are, by almost every working definition, the only pair of shoes in the house that everybody reaches for.

This is the strange and slightly miraculous thing about a really good pair of plain white sneakers. They are, in the strict catalog of the wardrobe, an athletic shoe. They were, originally, designed for running. They were made, by the people who invented them, to be worn on a tennis court, or a track, or the polished floor of a gymnasium. And yet, somewhere in the last ten or fifteen years, the white sneaker quietly stopped being an athletic shoe and became, instead, the most useful piece of footwear in the modern closet. It became the shoe you can wear to work. It became the shoe you can wear to a wedding. It became the shoe you can wear to walk the dog, to grab the mail, to wait in line at the post office, to meet a friend for the kind of coffee that runs an hour over time. The white sneaker, more than any other shoe in the world, has agreed to do all of it.

## The shoe that stops being a shoe

We tend to think of shoes as belonging to a category. A loafer is a shoe for a certain kind of day. A heel is a shoe for a certain kind of night. A boot is a shoe for a certain kind of weather. The white sneaker has refused, more or less entirely, to be categorized. The white sneaker will go with a suit. The white sneaker will go with a dress. The white sneaker will go with the oldest, softest pair of jeans in the closet, and will also go with the tailored trousers one wears to a meeting. The white sneaker has, in the last decade, become the closest thing the modern wardrobe has to a universal shoe.

This is, when you stop to think about it, an extraordinary thing. There has never, in the long history of footwear, been a shoe that has worked this hard. There have been shoes that were beautiful. There have been shoes that were comfortable. There have been shoes that were correct for one occasion or another. But the white sneaker is, by some distance, the first shoe to claim that it can be correct for all of them at once.

And the claim is, mostly, true. We have worn white sneakers to meetings where nobody blinked. We have worn white sneakers to dinners where nobody commented. We have worn white sneakers to the kind of event where, an hour into the evening, somebody has asked us, with what seemed like genuine surprise, whether those were new. The white sneaker has, by virtue of its plainness, become the most unobtrusive piece of footwear in the room. It disappears into the rest of the outfit. It does not ask to be admired. It asks only to be worn.

## What plainness gives back

There is, in the design of the modern white sneaker, a peculiar and rather lovely minimalism. The shoe is, in most versions of itself, almost entirely featureless. There is no obvious logo. There is no obvious hardware. There is no obvious stitching pattern that tries to draw attention to itself. The white sneaker is, in its best form, a small piece of curved leather with a rubber sole and a pair of laces. That is the whole shoe.

This plainness is not, as some people have suggested, an aesthetic strategy. It is, we think, a kind of permission. The white sneaker has agreed not to compete with anything else in the outfit. It has agreed to be the supporting piece. It has agreed to take up as little visual space as a shoe can take up and still be a shoe. And because it has agreed to all of these things, the white sneaker has, paradoxically, become the most versatile piece of footwear in the closet.

The lesson, here, is one we think the rest of the wardrobe could learn from. Plainness is not, in our experience, a compromise. Plainness is, when it is the right plainness, a gift to the things around it. A plain white sneaker lets the trousers be the trousers. A plain white sneaker lets the coat be the coat. A plain white sneaker lets the rest of the outfit be the rest of the outfit. The plainness is, in this sense, a kind of generosity.

### the small economy of one shoe

We have, in our own closets at various points in our lives, owned something close to a dozen pairs of shoes. We have had the polished loafers for the days when the loafers felt right. We have had the heeled boots for the days when the heeled boots felt right. We have had the running shoes for the days when the running shoes felt right. We have, in other words, accumulated a wardrobe of shoes the way some people accumulate a wardrobe of coats, by accumulating one for each occasion.

And then, at some point in the last ten years, we stopped doing that. We started, almost without noticing, reaching for the same pair of white sneakers. We started, almost without noticing, leaving the loafers in the closet, then leaving them in the back of the closet, then forgetting they were there. The white sneakers, by their plainness, had become the answer to almost every occasion that arrived.

This is the second miracle of the white sneaker. It is not just a shoe. It is, in a small way, an exit from the tyranny of having the right shoe. The white sneaker is the shoe that lets you stop thinking about shoes. The white sneaker is the shoe that lets a closet lose about half of its shoe floor. The white sneaker is the shoe that lets you, when you are packing for a trip, leave the second pair at home.

## The leather is the long version

Not all white sneakers are made equal. The canvas sneaker — of which there are several famous versions — is a perfectly good shoe. It is light. It is cheap. It washes in the washing machine. It has its place. But the canvas sneaker is, in our experience, a seasonal shoe. It is the shoe for the months when the ground is dry. It is the shoe for the part of the year when you do not mind if your socks get a little damp.

The leather white sneaker, by contrast, is the year-round shoe. The leather white sneaker is the shoe that has agreed to take a little rain. The leather white sneaker is the shoe that is still wearable, on a damp morning, in the kind of weather where the canvas version would have given up. The leather white sneaker is also, very often, the more comfortable shoe. A good leather upper will, over the course of a few weeks, agree to take the shape of the foot that is wearing it. A good leather lining will agree to breathe in a way a canvas lining does not. A good leather sole will, eventually, agree to be the most broken-in piece of footwear in the closet.

This is also why the leather white sneaker, more than almost any other shoe in the modern wardrobe, is worth spending a little more on. The leather sneaker is the shoe that will, with a small amount of care, last for several years. The leather sneaker is the shoe that will, with a small amount of cleaning, keep looking good through all of those years. The leather sneaker is the shoe that will, in the end, cost less per wear than the cheaper canvas version you would otherwise have replaced twice.

We would, of course, also say a small word about the sole. A good white sneaker has a sole that is, in some way, replaceable. There are cobblers, in most cities, who will, for a modest sum, replace the sole of a leather sneaker that has been worn to the welt. A cobbler who can do this is, in a small way, the most valuable ally the white sneaker has. A cobbler who can do this is the person who turns a two-year shoe into a six-year shoe.

## The shoe that does the work

We have, in this piece, been thinking of the white sneaker as a piece of fashion. But the white sneaker, in the end, is mostly not fashion. The white sneaker is, in fact, a piece of equipment. The white sneaker is the shoe that lets a person walk the long way to wherever they are going without thinking about their feet. The white sneaker is the shoe that lets a person stand for an hour at an outdoor concert without remembering that they are standing.

This is the third miracle of the white sneaker. It is a shoe that lets the wearer forget, almost entirely, that they are wearing shoes. The white sneaker, in its best form, has the quality of a piece of clothing that has become an extension of the foot rather than a covering for it. The white sneaker is the shoe you stop noticing by the second block.

This is also why the white sneaker has, in the last decade, so thoroughly taken over the wardrobe. People have been waiting for a shoe that would let them stop thinking about shoes. The white sneaker, by being both plain and well-made, has finally answered that.

## A small companion, by the door

The pair of white sneakers by the front door, in our experience, has become a small companion. They are the shoes we reach for when we do not know what the day is going to ask. They are the shoes we reach for when we want the day to be slightly easier. They are the shoes we reach for when we want, without quite admitting it, to be a little more ourselves than the more formal shoes of the closet allow.

This is the strange and rather wonderful thing about the right pair of white sneakers. They are not, by any standard of the wardrobe, the most interesting object in the closet. They are not the most expensive. They are not the most beautiful. They are not the most expressive. They are, instead, the most useful. They are the shoes that have agreed to do the work, day after day, without ever asking to be thanked.

And so we keep them by the door. And so we keep them, year after year, in roughly the same position. And so we keep them, even as the laces darken and the heel scuffs and the leather takes on the faint cream tone of something that has been worn with love. We keep them because they have, in their plain white way, earned the place. We keep them because they are, in fact, the shoes that have made almost all the other shoes in the closet quietly unnecessary.

This is the great economy of one good pair. This is what the white sneaker, of all the shoes in the closet, has agreed to offer. The cheap reinvention is not, in the end, a pair of sneakers. The cheap reinvention is the morning when you stop reaching for anything else.
