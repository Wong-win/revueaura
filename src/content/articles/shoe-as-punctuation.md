There is a moment, in the dressing of almost any outfit, when the room is essentially finished. The dress is on. The coat has been decided. The bag has been chosen. The earrings, if any, are at the ears. Everything, in other words, has been settled. And then, last of all, you turn to the shoes.

The shoes are not, strictly speaking, the most important part of the outfit. The shoes do not take up most of the visual space. The shoes do not, in most outfits, even take up most of the floor. But the shoes are *the last thing you decide*. And, as any writer will tell you, the last thing you decide is the thing that the sentence remembers.

This is what we mean, when we say the shoe is the punctuation. The shoe is the period at the end of the silhouette. The shoe is the slight, deliberate, slightly weighted thing that tells the room what kind of sentence the outfit has been. A comma? An exclamation mark? A long, considered ellipsis? The answer is in the shoe. The answer has been in the shoe all along.

It is the last decision because the shoe is the part of the outfit that touches the ground. It is the part that has to actually, physically, do the walking. And so it is the part that the wardrobe asks the most of, and the part that the wearer thinks about the longest. The shoe is the place where the outfit stops being an idea and becomes, in some practical sense, real.

This is also why the shoe is the part of the outfit that ages the most slowly, and the part that, when it ages, ages the most beautifully. The shoe has the longest working life of anything in the closet. The shoe has the closest relationship to the ground. The shoe is, in a small way, the most worn part of the outfit, and the most loved.

## The last sentence of the outfit

If the shoe is the punctuation, then different shoes make different sentences. There is the elegant, slightly melancholy sentence that ends in a flat leather sandal. There is the abrupt, slightly emphatic sentence that ends in a stiletto. There is the long, considered, slightly secretive sentence that ends in a clean white sneaker. There is the slightly tentative sentence that ends in a kitten heel. Each one is grammatical. Each one means something different. Each one ends the room in a slightly different way.

We think about this more than most people, perhaps. We have spent, over the years, an unusual amount of time thinking about shoes. We have come to believe that the shoes are, in many outfits, the only thing that matters. Not the only thing the eye sees — the dress and the bag see themselves first. But the only thing the eye *remembers*. The shoe is what the silhouette leans on, what the walk depends on, what the photograph, if there is a photograph, anchors itself to.

There is also, we have come to understand, a particular pleasure in choosing the shoe slowly. The pleasure is the pleasure of finishing something well. There is a kind of writer's pleasure in choosing the period at the end of the sentence. The sentence has been written. The sentence is, by most measures, complete. But the period — the small, deliberate mark — is what tells the reader the sentence is over. The shoe is the period. The shoe is the small, deliberate mark.

We have a friend who, when she is dressing for an evening, will always choose her shoes last. She will put the entire outfit on, look at herself in the mirror for a moment, and then walk back into the closet and try on three or four pairs before choosing the one. Sometimes the choice takes twenty minutes. She does not consider this excessive. She considers it the actual decision. The dress, by her lights, was decided weeks ago. The shoe is the present tense.

This is, we think, the right approach to the shoe. The shoe is not the supporting part of the outfit. The shoe is the part of the outfit that *finishes* the outfit. The shoe deserves the time.

## What the foot remembers

There is a small and rather wonderful thing about the shoe, which is that the foot knows the shoe before the eye does. The foot knows within about thirty seconds of putting the shoe on whether the shoe is right. The heel is correctly set. The toe box is correctly shaped. The arch is correctly supported. The break of the shoe, across the ball of the foot, is correctly placed. None of this is visible in the mirror. All of it is felt in the foot.

A good shoe is, almost by definition, a shoe that the foot forgets it is wearing. The shoe becomes part of the standing. The shoe becomes part of the walking. The shoe disappears into the body, in a way that the rest of the outfit does not disappear. You remember you are wearing the dress. You remember you are wearing the coat. You do not remember you are wearing the shoe, because the shoe has agreed to be part of the floor.

A bad shoe is the opposite. A bad shoe is a shoe that interrupts the floor. A bad shoe is a shoe that the foot cannot forget. The heel slips. The toe pinches. The arch is not quite there. The break is in the wrong place. The shoe, instead of becoming part of the body, becomes a small, persistent reminder of being on the body. And a small, persistent reminder of being on the body is the very last thing you want from a piece of clothing.

This is why the shoe is the most conservative part of the wardrobe. Not fashionable, conservative — but careful, conservative. You do not, in our experience, buy the very trendy shoe. You buy the very good shoe. You buy the shoe from a maker who has been making shoes for forty years. You buy the shoe in a leather that will age well. You buy the shoe at a price that allows the maker to take the time the shoe requires. And then you wear the shoe for a long, long time.

The shoe is also the part of the wardrobe you should be willing to mend. A good shoe, well cared for, can be resoled two or three times. A good shoe can be reheeled. A good shoe can have its linings replaced. A good shoe, properly maintained, will outlive most of the other things in the closet. It will become, after a decade, almost part of the family.

## Choosing the period

If we were to offer a small set of notes on how to choose the shoe, in 2026, they would be these. There are only a few, because the shoe does not require many.

Choose the shoe for the walk, not the photograph. There is a particular shoe — and we have all owned at least one of these — that looks beautiful in the mirror and that, twenty minutes into the evening, has made the entire foot ache. This is the wrong shoe, even if it is the right shoe in the photograph. The shoe is a working piece of clothing. The shoe will be asked to do actual work all evening. The shoe should be chosen for the work.

Choose the shoe in the color of your life, not the color of your outfit. Black is safe. Brown is warm. Cream is brave. There is no objectively right color of shoe. There is the color of shoe that goes with the most pairs of trousers you actually wear, and that color is, almost always, the answer.

Choose the shoe that can be repaired. The shoe that cannot be repaired is the shoe you will replace in two years. The shoe that can be repaired is the shoe you will own for twenty. A small investment in a cobbler, in the third year of the shoe's life, will buy you another ten years of walking. This is, in our experience, one of the most underrated small economies in the modern wardrobe.

Choose the shoe quietly. The loud shoe is the shoe that is trying to do the work of the dress. The dress should be allowed to do the work of the dress. The shoe should be allowed to do the work of the shoe. The shoe should sit at the bottom of the silhouette the way a comma sits at the bottom of a sentence — small, necessary, almost invisible, and utterly crucial to the meaning.

And finally, choose the shoe for the version of the evening you actually want to have. The stiletto says *I am prepared for the room to be difficult*. The kitten heel says *I am prepared for the room to be long*. The flat sandal says *I am prepared for the room to be warm*. The clean white sneaker says *I am prepared for the room to be ordinary, and I am at peace with that*. Each of these is a fine evening. Each of these is a sentence. Choose the period that fits the sentence you want to have written.

## The closing gesture

We have come, over the years, to think of putting on the shoes as the closing gesture of the dressing. Not the opening gesture of the leaving. The closing gesture of the dressing. The shoes are the last thing the room sees. The shoes are the last thing you do, in the morning, before you walk out of the house. The shoes are the small, final, slightly weighted punctuation that tells the day what kind of day it is going to be.

There is a small pleasure in this. The pleasure is the pleasure of having arrived, in the dressing, at the place where you can stop deciding. The shoe says *we are done*. The shoe says *the sentence is finished*. The shoe says *walk out into the day, and let the day begin*.

It is not the most important part of the outfit. It is not the most photographed part of the outfit. It is not, in any single visual sense, the most striking part of the outfit. But it is the last part. And, as any careful reader will tell you, the last part is the part the room remembers.

The shoe, then. The small, deliberate, slightly weighted punctuation at the end of the silhouette. The period that lets the walk begin. The closing gesture that the dressing has been working toward, all morning, without quite saying so.

We have a deep fondness for the shoe. We have, in particular, a fondness for the well-worn shoe. The shoe that has been walked in for years, that has been walked in for cities, that has been walked in for dinners and weddings and long afternoons and slow Sunday mornings. The shoe that has become, through walking, almost part of the ground it walks on.

This is the shoe we would like to wear, when the evening is over and the room is empty and the day, finally, begins to fade. The well-worn shoe. The small closure. The slight, deliberate, slightly weighted period at the end of a day that has, we hope, been a sentence worth ending.
